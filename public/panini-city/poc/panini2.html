About Panini Projection

Panini projection is a cylindrical-perspective family designed for very wide fields of view. Compared with standard rectilinear perspective, it reduces edge stretching and keeps many verticals visually straighter, which is why it is popular for architectural and game-camera wide shots.

The modern graphics formulation is commonly associated with Sharpless, Smid, and Salama (2010), building on earlier “vedutismo” perspective practices from wide city views. It has been used in engines and game rendering pipelines as a practical wide-FOV post effect.

This demo renders a normal perspective scene first, then applies Panini in a fullscreen shader using inverse mapping per pixel (not per-vertex warping). The D control adjusts projection strength, DPR Multiplier raises source sampling resolution to reduce post-warp jaggies, and corner-safe vertical fitting keeps the screen filled.
